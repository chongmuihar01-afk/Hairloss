Thinning Hair? 5 Simple Hair-Care Tips for Fuller-Looking, Healthier Hair

Noticing more hair on your brush or feeling that your hair looks thinner than before? You are not alone. Changes in hair appearance can happen for many reasons, and a good hair-care routine can help keep your scalp and hair in good condition.

From choosing a gentle shampoo to avoiding excessive heat styling, small changes to your daily routine can make a difference in how your hair looks and feels. Here are five practical habits for healthier, fuller-looking hair.

1. Keep your scalp clean — without over-washing

A healthy-looking head of hair starts with regular scalp care. Wash often enough to remove excess oil, sweat and styling-product build-up, but choose a routine that suits your hair type. Use lukewarm rather than very hot water and massage shampoo gently with your fingertips instead of scratching the scalp.

2. Choose a gentle shampoo

When hair feels fine or fragile, a gentle cleansing routine can help it feel fresh without unnecessary rough handling. Concentrate shampoo mainly on the scalp, then let the lather rinse through the lengths. Follow with conditioner where needed, especially on dry ends.

3. Be gentle with wet hair

Wet hair can be easier to stretch and damage. Instead of rubbing vigorously with a towel, gently squeeze out excess water and pat the hair dry. Detangle carefully with a wide-tooth comb, starting at the ends and gradually working upward.

4. Reduce excessive heat and tight styling

Frequent high heat from hair dryers, straighteners and curling tools can leave hair feeling dry or brittle. Use a lower heat setting when possible and give your hair regular breaks. Avoid repeatedly pulling hair into very tight styles, and choose gentler accessories that do not snag.

5. Support your hair with consistent everyday care

Hair care works best as a routine rather than a one-day fix. Handle your hair gently, eat a balanced diet, manage styling habits and pay attention to changes in your scalp or hair. If you experience sudden, severe or unexplained hair loss, consider speaking with a healthcare professional.
